How it works

Chords
Each chord is a major or minor triad. The next one is reached by moving one or two notes by a semitone or tone, so every change is smooth, but at high Unease the moves chosen lead to chords that share no key (C major to A♭ minor is the classic film-score "something is wrong" change).
Colour
Each chord may be dressed with an added ninth or a seventh, or have its third replaced by a second or a fourth (sus2, sus4). A sus chord is neither major nor minor, which suits a place you cannot read.
Voices
Five voices never stop. Each slides to its nearest note in the new chord a few seconds apart, so the old and new chords overlap.
Waveforms
Each voice has six layers: a pair of saws (bright), a triangle (soft), a square (hollow), a glassy wave made only of odd partials, a reedy wave with a nasal peak, and a sine an octave up (air). Drift chooses which one or two lead in each voice; the line under the chord name shows the five voices' current blends.
Drift
Each voice has its own filter, swept by a slow wave with a period between 47 and 131 seconds, all different, so the voices open and close out of step. Every 9 seconds or so one voice is also given a new blend of waveforms, a new detune, a new place in the stereo field and a new filter edge, and glides there over about 20 seconds.
Drone
Two low sine waves a fraction of a tone apart, which makes a slow beat. It stays on a note the old and new chords share whenever it can.
Bells
Three loops of 17.3, 23.7 and 31.1 seconds. Their lengths never line up, so the pattern does not repeat.
Arpeggio
Now and again a short plucked run climbs through the notes of the chord sounding at that moment, in time with the heartbeat (three or four notes to a beat), fading in and out and trailing an echo. The first one comes within about 15 seconds; after that the wait is random, set by the slider.
Heartbeat
The only thing here with a tempo. Two low thumps, the second softer and about a third of a beat later, each a sine wave whose pitch falls quickly from about 95 to 42 cycles a second. The gap between beats wanders by a few percent, as a real pulse does. The chords and bells still ignore it.
Room
One long echo made from decaying noise. In the game its length would come from each room's size.
